Design direction · insolvency flags on the company chart

Surfacing an individual's insolvency history without leaving the chart

You pushed back on my false-positive concern and you were mostly right. This is the revised proposal: flag everything, show the evidence with the flag, and let the tier say how the match was made rather than hiding matches behind caution.

前Where I was wrong, and what actually differs

My worry was that auto-matching "John ABC" against the insolvency register would flag the wrong person. Your answer: the individual search already does exactly that, with the same API and the same name matching, and you read it fine. That's correct, and it makes most of my caution inconsistent — I was proposing to suppress on the chart what we already show without hesitation one screen over.

Two things do genuinely differ, and they're narrower than what I originally said:

  • Solicited versus unsolicited. When you type "John ABC" you know who you're looking for and you're making the identification yourself. On a chart the app goes and looks without being asked, then puts a mark on a person. That's a different act, even with identical data behind it.
  • Volume changes how carefully you read. One search is one conscious judgement. Forty directors is forty judgements you'll make while skimming, and common names mean at least one will be wrong on a decent-sized chart.

So the fix isn't to suppress — it's to make the chart flag carry the same evidence the person search gives you. A bare red badge is a verdict. A badge that opens the estate name, type, adjudication date, status and address is the same judgement call you already make, just surfaced earlier. That's the whole redesign.

証And we can corroborate better than name alone

Digging through this I found something that raises the ceiling. Director records from the companies register carry physicalAddress, and insolvency records carry addressAtAdjudication. KydVerificationPanel already does address comparison for a related purpose, so the machinery exists.

That means a name match backed by a matching address is materially stronger than a name match alone — and we can say which one we have, rather than treating every match as equally uncertain. The tier isn't a caution level. It's a statement of how the match was made.

術How it runs

  • Linked flags are computed on graph build. Zero new requests — the chart already knows which companies are insolvent and which people are attached to them. This half ships regardless.
  • Personal checks run in the background after the chart renders, deduped by normalised name (charts repeat directors constantly), concurrency-limited, and cached for the session. The chart appears immediately and flags develop over a few seconds — the same progressive pattern App already uses when it enriches person results.
  • Nothing blocks. A failed check leaves the person unflagged rather than erroring the chart, and the case file says how many are still pending.

決Three things I need from you

  1. Automatic, or a button? Automatic is the better experience and is what you asked for. It costs two API calls per unique individual — a forty-director chart is roughly eighty extra calls per search. If that's fine on your quota, automatic. If not, a "check individuals" action makes the cost a deliberate choice.
  2. Does amber go into the exported chart? My instinct is that linked and corroborated export, and name match stays in the app — a bare unverified badge in a document someone else reads is the one place a false positive does real damage, because the evidence card doesn't travel with it. But you know your readers; if the export carries the evidence card too, I'd happily export all three.
  3. Discharged records — flag or not? A bankruptcy discharged in 2009 is a different signal from a current one. I'd show both but render discharged ones in amber regardless of corroboration, since currency matters more than confidence there. Say if you'd rather they were identical.
