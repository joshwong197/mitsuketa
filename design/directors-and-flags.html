You were right on both counts. I confirmed the first in the code rather than the screenshot, and it turns out the director data is already being fetched and thrown away. The second is a layout fault with a straightforward fix that doesn't depend on scroll behaviour.

欠Confirmed: the graph is shareholders only

apiService.ts builds the upstream crawl from shareholdings.shareAllocation → shareholder. Directors never become nodes. There is a details.roles branch at line 334 — but it's an else if, so it only runs when a company has no shareholdings at all. It exists to catch General Partners of a Limited Partnership, not directors of a normal company.

And the data is already in hand. The NZBN entity response carries a roles collection — "the persons and business entities that are directors, trustees, partners, officers" — and fetchEntityDetailsFull already returns it. Same shape as the property estates: fetched on every crawl, then discarded. Adding directors costs no extra API calls at all.

This also makes the insolvency-flag work land properly. Flagging individuals matters most for the people with control, and right now those people aren't on the chart — only the ones who own shares. On Eco-Smart that's the whole story: Ritesh Mani appears as a director.

How directors are kept from swamping the chart

  • A dashed leading ring and a dashed edge. Directors control; shareholders own. Making that visually distinct means a chart with both doesn't read as one undifferentiated crowd — you can see at a glance which people hold shares and which run the thing.
  • "Director" replaces "Individual" on the pill, so the role is stated rather than inferred from edge style alone.
  • A toolbar toggle, on by default. Shareholders / Directors / Ceased roles, independently. A company with nine directors can be collapsed back to the ownership view in one click, which is the anti-obfuscation escape hatch you asked for.
  • Ceased roles stay off by default. The roles collection includes resigned directors; showing them always would double the person count on any long-lived company.

Why this shape

  • Collapsed by default, not scroll-driven. You tried auto-collapse on scroll and it didn't hold up — that's because scroll-linked collapse is fighting the browser and fails hardest exactly when content is tall. A default-collapsed disclosure is deterministic: it's the same height every time, on every screen, with no nested scrollbar.
  • The summary line already answers the question. "2 records · both discharged · 2002, 2011" plus No current is the whole triage. You only expand when the answer matters.
  • Currency is called out separately from count, because a current bankruptcy and a discharged one from 2002 are different facts and the old cards weighted them identically.
  • The timeline is the creative bit you asked for. Two dates in a list don't tell you anything; two adjudication marks against the span of directorships answer the question a reader actually has — was he running companies while bankrupt? Both marks fall inside the band, which is the finding. It's derived purely from dates we already hold.
  • Same strip serves disqualification and the clear state — one row saying "Register checks · clear" rather than a full card announcing nothing.

同One person, one node — and a bug underneath

Yes, and it should be one node regardless of how it looks, because the current behaviour is wrong. apiService.ts mints person node ids like this:

holderId = `IND-${'$'}{holderLabel.replace(/\s+/g, '-')}-${'$'}{Math.random().toString(36).substr(2, 5)}`

A random suffix, so people can never deduplicate. The guard on the next line — if (this.nodes.has(holderId)) continue — can never fire for a person. That means someone holding shares in two subsidiaries already appears as two separate nodes today, before directors enter the picture at all.

And the app already assumes otherwise. Case notes are keyed by nzbn ?? personId(label), and compareService exports a proper stable personId() that normalises and sorts name tokens. Adding a note to a duplicated person highlights every copy of them, because the note system was built expecting the identity the graph never assigned.

So the fix is to use the personId() that already exists. One node per person, and their relationships become edges — which is the correct model anyway: a role belongs to the link between a person and a company, not to the person. Anjali Mani directing one company and holding shares in another is two edges from one node, not two Anjalis.

That also turns the chart into something more useful. Once people deduplicate you can see at a glance that one individual controls three of five subsidiaries — which is invisible today, because they're drawn as three unrelated pills.

The half-and-half mark

Rather than invent a new glyph, the ring does it. Directors already get a dashed leading ring and shareholders a solid one, so someone who is both gets a ring that is literally half of each — solid arc on the left for ownership, dashed on the right for control. It's two SVG arcs on one circle, and it reads at 30px where two kanji crammed into a square would not.

Both role characters then sit together on the role line — 株締 — with the detail spelled out in words beside them. The mark is the signal; the text carries the content. Look at Anjali Mani on the chart above: one node, split ring, two labelled edges to the target.

The node shows the union of roles across the whole chart; the edges carry the specifics. If someone directs company A and holds shares in company B, the pill says 株締 and the two edges say which is which. That keeps the node honest without pretending every relationship is the same.

期The dates check out — and there are more than two

You asked whether the register exposes both ends. It does, and the official schema in docs/insolvency-trustee-services.json carries four more fields we currently declare nowhere:

FieldWhat it gives usRead today?
adjudicationOrLiquidationDateStart of the bankruptcyyes
dischargeOrCompletionDateEnd of ityes
dischargeOrCompletionTypeReason for dischargeno
dischargeConditionExpiryDateWhen discharge conditions lapse — this is what "(conditional) discharged" is pointing atno
dischargeSuspendedBoolean. A suspended discharge means still bankruptno
annulmentDateBankruptcy annulled rather than dischargedno

So the timeline becomes a span, not a point — I've redrawn it above. Solid bar is the bankruptcy period, hatched tail is the conditional-discharge window from dischargeConditionExpiryDate.

And that changes what the timeline is for. Two dots told you when something happened. Two bars laid against the directorship band show whether the person held directorships during a bankruptcy — and an undischarged bankrupt is prohibited from managing a company in New Zealand. On this record both periods fall inside the band.

The app should state that as a mechanical overlap and nothing more — "2 directorships held during a bankruptcy period" — never as a conclusion that anything was breached. Dates overlap for innocent reasons, the register lags, and that call is the reader's. But it's exactly the kind of thing a person would otherwise have to work out on paper, and we already hold every date needed to compute it.

dischargeSuspended is worth wiring on its own merits: a suspended discharge means the person is still bankrupt regardless of what insolvencyStatus reads, which is precisely the case where an amber "historic" flag would be wrong.

株Different kanji for the two roles

Agreed, and it costs nothing since the pill already has a role line. Taking the key character of each register's own term:

MarkRoleFrom
株Shareholder 株主 kabunushi — shareholder. The character means share/stock outright.
締Director 取締役 torishimariyaku — company director.

I deliberately avoided 役, which would be the obvious pick from 役員, because the property report already uses it for easements (地役権). One glyph meaning two unrelated things in one app is the kind of thing that reads fine on the day and confuses everyone later.

Placement: the glyph sits on the role line rather than replacing 印 in the ring, so the inkan stays the person mark and the role reads as an attribute. On a flagged pill the role glyph survives next to the flag — Ritesh Mani above shows 締 紅 together, so you don't lose the role to the alert.

決Decisions

  1. Directors on by default? I've drawn it on, with a toggle. On is right for due-diligence, but it does grow the person count on every chart — say if you'd rather it defaulted off with the toggle to bring them in.
  2. Do directors get crawled, or just drawn? Drawing them is free. But if a director is also a shareholder of something else, do we follow that upstream like we do for corporate shareholders? Following makes the chart far more revealing and considerably bigger.
  3. Does the summary strip expand automatically when there's a current insolvency? My instinct is yes — collapsed for historic, open for current, since that's the case where you want the detail without asking. dischargeSuspended should count as current.
  4. Does the overlap count get stated? "2 directorships held during a bankruptcy period" on the summary line, computed from dates we already hold. I think yes, phrased mechanically — but it is the single most consequential sentence the app would ever generate, so it is your call rather than mine.
