読Does it read?

  • Three flags among thirteen nodes. Sparse enough that the eye finds them, which is the whole test. If a chart lit up half its people the marks would stop meaning anything.
  • The person never outranks the event. John Abercrombie is amber because the companies causing his flag are amber. Peter Rawiri is red because there's a record against him. That reads correctly left to right.
  • Clean individuals are untouched — same pill, same "Individual" line as today. No "no records found" badge, because absence of a flag already says it.
  • The flag replaces the "Individual" line rather than adding a row, so pills don't grow and the layout doesn't reflow when flags arrive a few seconds after the chart.
  • Penrose Storage shows the existing convention working — removed, and 紅 PREV: LIQUIDATION. Nothing about the company treatment changes.
  • The dashed wash edges from Abercrombie to the two insolvent subsidiaries are the existing sibling-edge style, so the reason for his flag is visible on the canvas.

決Still need from you

  1. Automatic, or a button? The two free tiers cost nothing and I'd always compute them. The personal checks are two calls per unique individual — roughly eighty extra on a forty-director chart. Automatic is the better experience; a button makes the cost a deliberate choice.
  2. Does amber "name match" go into the exported chart? The evidence card doesn't travel with an exported PNG, so an unverified badge stands alone in a document someone else reads. I'd export the red tiers and the linked-company amber, and hold back name-match — but say if you'd rather it all went.
  3. Discharged records. Peter Rawiri red because it's corroborated, even though discharged in 2011? Or does currency demote it to amber? I've drawn it red here on the basis that the record is real and the reader can see the date.
